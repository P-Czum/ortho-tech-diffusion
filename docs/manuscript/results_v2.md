### 2026-09-03 · material 66 (61 after naming review, pending) · counts from the 66-group re-run · all numbers from `osie_ostateczne.csv` and `wycofania_mechanizmy.csv` · Table 1 (76 rows) in Supplement S5

## What emerged, and when

Sixty-six technologies and techniques emerged in the orthopaedic literature between 2000 and 2025 (Table 1, Figure 1). Eleven date from the first decade, 42 from the second, and 13 from 2020–2023; the last figure is truncated by design, because emergence requires three confirmatory years and no technology can be counted after 2023.

The earliest were hip resurfacing, kyphoplasty, and total disc replacement (all 2005), followed by autologous chondrocyte implantation, computer navigation, locking plates, and the Ponseti method (2006), double-bundle ACL reconstruction, reverse shoulder arthroplasty, and total ankle arthroplasty (2007). The second decade brought percutaneous and endoscopic spine surgery (percutaneous kyphoplasty 2010, transforaminal interbody fusion 2010, lateral interbody fusion 2013, endoscopic discectomy 2016, oblique interbody fusion 2018), patient-specific instrumentation (2012), the direct anterior approach (2014), 3D printing (2016), and mesenchymal stem cells (2017). Since 2019: artificial intelligence and machine learning (2019), robotic assistance (2020), femoral neck system and targeted muscle reinnervation (2021), augmented reality and kinematic alignment (2022).

Nineteen of the 66 — nearly a third — had not yet peaked by 2025, among them reverse shoulder arthroplasty, robotic assistance, artificial intelligence, 3D printing, patient-specific instrumentation, and kinematic alignment.

## Names coined by the arrival of a rival

Three phrases that the detector reported as emerging technologies were not new techniques but new names for existing ones (Table 4). Single-bundle ACL reconstruction had been the standard for decades and needed no adjective until double-bundle reconstruction appeared in 2007; three years later, comparisons required a name for the old technique, and *single bundle* emerged. Anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty was named against reverse arthroplasty; mechanical alignment against kinematic alignment. In each case the only new thing in the event was the rival.

These names were excluded from the material and are shown here because they mark where an emergence detector measures language rather than technology — and the two part company precisely when competition appears. Without specialist review, *single bundle* would have stood in Table 1 as a technology of 2010 and its later decline in Table 3 as a withdrawal, and both would have been false.

## Where, and how fast

Research on these technologies was concentrated to very different degrees (Table 2, Figure 2). The effective number of contributing countries ranged from 1.2 to 14.7.

Thirteen technologies were single-country literatures (effective number ≤ 2.5), and in every one the country was China (seven) or the United States (six). Percutaneous kyphoplasty (545 papers, 90% from China), percutaneous endoscopic lumbar discectomy (81% China), femoral neck system (70% China), proximal femoral nail antirotation (69% China), and oblique lumbar interbody fusion (65% China) on one side; primary hip arthroscopy (89% USA), targeted muscle reinnervation (68% USA), manipulation under anaesthesia (67% USA), and electronic medical records (66% USA) on the other.

Twelve technologies were studied across nine or more effective countries, with no country above 24%: cone-beam CT (14.7 countries), short-stem hip arthroplasty and volar locking plates (12.8), patient-specific instrumentation (12.0), mesenchymal stem cells (11.1), the anterolateral ligament (10.2), locking plates (10.0), minimally invasive plate osteosynthesis (9.7), cephalomedullary nails and medial unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (9.5), and the Latarjet procedure (9.2).

Paper counts did not distinguish the two groups: percutaneous kyphoplasty and cone-beam CT had 545 and 553 papers respectively.

Doubling time of attention could be computed for 55 technologies; for 11 the peak came within two years of emergence or growth was not sustained. The fastest were primary hip arthroscopy (1.7 years), robotic assistance (2.0), artificial intelligence (2.1), oblique lumbar interbody fusion (2.2), femoral neck system (2.3), and hip resurfacing (2.4). Six technologies grew so slowly that doubling exceeded 20 years — total disc replacement, polyetheretherketone, short-stem hips, cervical disc arthroplasty, total elbow arthroplasty, and cephalomedullary nails.

## Who leads: two countries, and the country of origin

**Figure 2.** Number of technologies for which each country leads the literature (first-author affiliation, papers from emergence onward); for countries leading on one or two technologies, the technology is named. `figures/fig_countries.{png,svg}`.

The leading country was the United States or China for 61 of the 66 technologies. The five exceptions each point to where the technology came from: dual-mobility cups (France, 32% of papers — the design of Bousquet, Saint-Étienne), short-stem hip arthroplasty (Germany), volar locking plates (Japan), and minimally invasive plate osteosynthesis and open-wedge high tibial osteotomy (South Korea). Outside the two largest producers of orthopaedic literature, a country leads the research on a technology only when the technology originated there — five cases of five.

## Why attention fades

Twelve technologies lost more than half of their peak attention (Table 3, Figure 3). Because they peaked in different years — computer navigation in 2007, dual-mobility cups in 2020 — persistence was measured relative to each technology's own peak, at 1–3, 3–5, and 5–7 years after it.

The two technologies whose evidence turned against use fell faster and deeper than all ten others, in every window: metal-on-metal bearings and hip resurfacing retained 0.27–0.48 of peak attention at 1–3 years, 0.17–0.25 at 3–5, and 0.11–0.17 at 5–7, against 0.50–0.87, 0.35–0.73, and 0.26–0.72 for the rest. Within the ten, the state of evidence did not separate: technologies with an unresolved question (double-bundle ACL reconstruction, autologous chondrocyte implantation, total disc replacement, microfracture) overlapped entirely with those adopted into routine care (kyphoplasty, the Ponseti method, dual-mobility cups). The literature registers a negative verdict; it does not register a positive one. Two cases show attention falling without rejection: computer navigation retained 0.66 of peak at 3–5 years as it was absorbed into robotic systems, and mesenchymal stem cells 0.73 as the topic moved and returned.

Eleven of 24 technologies initially identified as declining were changes of name rather than of practice — kyphoplasty and balloon kyphoplasty, autologous chondrocyte implantation and its abbreviation — and one, single-bundle ACL reconstruction, was a retronym (Table 4). They were removed before analysis.
