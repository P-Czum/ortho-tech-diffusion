## What emerged, and when

Seventy-six technologies and techniques emerged in the orthopaedic literature between 2000 and 2025 (Table 1, Figure 1). Twelve date from the first decade, 49 from the second, and 15 from 2020–2023; the last figure is truncated by design, because emergence requires three confirmatory years and no technology can be counted after 2023.

The earliest were hip resurfacing, kyphoplasty, and total disc replacement (all 2005), followed by autologous chondrocyte implantation, computer navigation, locking plates, and the Ponseti method (2006), double-bundle ACL reconstruction, reverse shoulder arthroplasty, and total ankle arthroplasty (2007). The second decade brought percutaneous and endoscopic spine surgery (percutaneous kyphoplasty 2010, transforaminal interbody fusion 2010, lateral interbody fusion 2013, endoscopic discectomy 2016, oblique interbody fusion 2018), patient-specific instrumentation (2012), the direct anterior approach (2014), 3D printing (2016), and mesenchymal stem cells (2017). Since 2019: artificial intelligence and machine learning (2019), robotic assistance (2020), femoral neck system and targeted muscle reinnervation (2021), augmented reality and kinematic alignment (2022).

Twenty-three of the 76 had not yet peaked by 2025 — among them reverse shoulder arthroplasty, robotic assistance, artificial intelligence, 3D printing, patient-specific instrumentation, and kinematic alignment.

## Where, and how fast

Research on these technologies was concentrated to very different degrees (Table 2, Figure 2). The effective number of contributing countries ranged from 1.2 to 14.7.

Fourteen technologies were single-country literatures (effective number ≤ 2.5), and in every one the country was China or the United States. Percutaneous kyphoplasty (545 papers, 90% from China), percutaneous endoscopic lumbar discectomy (81% China), oblique lumbar interbody fusion (65% China), femoral neck system (70% China), and proximal femoral nail antirotation (69% China) on one side; primary hip arthroscopy (89% USA), anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty (76% USA), targeted muscle reinnervation (68% USA), manipulation under anaesthesia (67% USA), and electronic medical records (66% USA) on the other.

Thirteen technologies were studied across nine or more effective countries, with no country above 24%: cone-beam CT (14.7 countries), short-stem hip arthroplasty and volar locking plates (12.8), mechanical alignment (12.7), patient-specific instrumentation (12.0), mesenchymal stem cells (11.1), locking plates (10.0), minimally invasive plate osteosynthesis (9.7), cephalomedullary nails and medial unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (9.5), and the Latarjet procedure (9.2).

Paper counts did not distinguish the two groups: percutaneous kyphoplasty and cone-beam CT had 545 and 553 papers respectively.

Doubling time of attention could be computed for 61 technologies. The fastest were primary hip arthroscopy (1.7 years), robotic assistance (2.0), artificial intelligence (2.1), oblique lumbar interbody fusion (2.2), femoral neck system (2.3), and hip resurfacing (2.4). Seven technologies grew so slowly that doubling exceeded 20 years — total disc replacement, polyetheretherketone, short-stem hips, cervical disc arthroplasty, total elbow arthroplasty, cephalomedullary nails, and single-bundle ACL reconstruction.

## Why attention fades

Twelve technologies lost more than half of their peak attention (Table 3, Figure 3). Because they peaked in different years — computer navigation in 2007, dual-mobility cups in 2020 — persistence was measured relative to each technology's own peak, at 1–3, 3–5, and 5–7 years after it.

The two technologies whose evidence turned against use fell faster and deeper than all ten others, in every window: metal-on-metal bearings and hip resurfacing retained 0.27–0.48 of peak attention at 1–3 years, 0.17–0.25 at 3–5, and 0.11–0.17 at 5–7, against 0.50–0.87, 0.35–0.73, and 0.26–0.72 for the rest. Within the ten, the state of evidence did not separate: technologies with an unresolved question (double-bundle ACL reconstruction, autologous chondrocyte implantation, total disc replacement, microfracture) overlapped entirely with those adopted into routine care (kyphoplasty, the Ponseti method, dual-mobility cups). Two cases illustrate why attention can fall without rejection: computer navigation retained 0.66 of peak at 3–5 years as it was absorbed into robotic systems, and mesenchymal stem cells 0.73 as the topic moved and returned.

Eleven of 24 technologies initially identified as declining were changes of name rather than of practice — kyphoplasty and balloon kyphoplasty, autologous chondrocyte implantation and its abbreviation — and one, single-bundle ACL reconstruction, was a retronym: a name coined for the existing standard when double-bundle reconstruction appeared. They were removed before analysis.
